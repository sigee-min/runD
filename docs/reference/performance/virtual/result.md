## Independent Terminals Versus Batching

A two-command experiment reduced submission count but serialized both bank
terminals. Reverting to independent bank terminals produced a mean paired p50
ratio of `0.90265`; independent terminals won all three packets in 21 of 32
multi-epoch cells, batching won 8, and 3 were mixed. Production therefore
retains independent terminals.

This comparison shows only that the tested batching shape was worse on this
surface. It does not prove that every batching design is slower or that a
future compound native DAG preserving intermediate terminal edges cannot win.

## Coherent Output

Against the immediately preceding independent-terminal packet, the coherent
Device-output path preserved output, identity, backing bytes, and traffic
contracts across all 240 paired rows. Mean Metal readback fell from
`143.834 us` to zero. Mean Metal warm p50 fell from `496.446 us` to
`450.693 us`; the ratio of means is `0.907839`, and the mean of individual
paired ratios is `0.862772`. Sixty-one of 80 cells were faster in all three
packets, none was slower in all three, and 19 were mixed.

Native submit wait was essentially unchanged (`410.672 us` before,
`412.901 us` after). The evidence therefore supports removal of Host output
copy/readback work on this hardware. It does not support a claim that
per-epoch submission overhead, GPU-driven transfer scheduling, or a native
Persistent/Host/Device DAG is solved.

All 240 robust crossover labels remained `cpu_observed`; no CPU→Metal bracket
was observed anywhere in the frozen grid. This is a bounded statement about
the measured `(N,r,active ratio)` surface, not a universal CPU winner.

admitting Host-memory Window into the recurrent owner. All packets contained
240 accepted paired rows. The recurrent packet reduced mean Metal backing
reads from `46,939.1` to `39,338.5` bytes (`16.2%`) and the largest cell from

from `430.231 us` to `764.892 us`; the largest `N=262,143,r=128` observations
